# Master Prompt for Claude — Ultra-Photorealistic Tactical FPS

## Mission and Creative Freedom

Build, run, verify, and deliver a finished real-time tactical first-person shooter. The visual target is ultra-photorealistic AAA quality at normal gameplay speed, from the opening spawn through an entire match. Make one exceptional map and one exceptional primary weapon. Give both the close-up craftsmanship and the stable, responsive presentation expected of a premium FPS. Spend production effort on what the player sees, hears, and controls during the match.

You have full authority over the engine, target platform, graphics API, language, libraries, asset packages, middleware, production tools, test tools, project layout, and implementation. Choose the combination that can produce the best-looking playable result and that you can build, run, and inspect in the available environment. Native, browser, and other viable delivery routes are all eligible. Use engine systems, plugins, templates, external production tools, commercial or freely licensed assets, scans, motion capture, animation clips, recorded audio, synthesis, procedural generation, and original work as useful. Integrate and tailor them to one cohesive game. Named techniques and example values in this brief describe useful options or targets. Choose any implementation that delivers the required behavior and image quality.

Use assets you create, assets supplied with appropriate rights, and assets whose terms allow the intended use and distribution. Adapt the art under a consistent direction and give the game its own identity. Keep a manifest with the source, creator or provider, license, attribution, and redistribution terms as applicable for each external asset or pack used. Include required notices. Choose accessible equivalents if a dependency, asset, or service is unavailable. Package the complete bot match, audio, and visuals for local offline play after setup. Include all required runtime resources locally and make zero external requests during play.

If a reference video is supplied, examine representative moments across the clip. Match its forward market-street framing, sunlit urban density, sky and clouds, weapon scale and presence, encounter pace, readable combat feedback, and restrained cinematic treatment. ## Match and Player Controls

- Ship a complete offline team deathmatch on one densely detailed urban map. Put the player and AI teammates against an AI-controlled enemy team. Default to 4v4 or 5v5, a ten-minute time limit, and a 30-elimination team score limit. Spawn the player promptly into the sunlit district, award team score for eliminations, end the match at the score limit or timeout, show the result, and support an in-game restart that resets match state.
- Start the player with a detailed modern carbine, a 30-round magazine, reserve ammunition, one fictional tactical throwable, and one fictional explosive throwable. Give both items a working throw, trajectory and collision, audible arrival, distinct readable effect, and inventory consumption shown on the HUD. Give the tactical item a utility effect the player and bots can respond to and the explosive item a fictional damage effect that participates in combat and scoring.
- Support walk, sprint, crouch, jump, short mantle, aim down sights (ADS), fire, fire-mode selection, reload, weapon inspect, throwable selection and use, pause, and respawn. Capture mouse look for responsive low-latency aiming, with raw input where supported. Expose sensitivity, field of view (FOV), ADS sensitivity multiplier, invert-Y, and key rebinding.
- Tune acceleration, deceleration, slopes, steps, crouch transitions, landing compression, and subtle camera inertia for quick, grounded movement and precise aim. Recognize body, limb, and head hits with fictional armor-neutral damage values. Connect damage, hit and kill feedback, team scoring, death, ragdoll, and respawn. Keep impacts and death reactions non-graphic and free of gore or dismemberment.

## World, Art Direction, and Environment

### Map and combat flow

Build one compact urban map approximately 120–180 meters across. Set it in a bright, war-worn market district inspired by North Africa or the eastern Mediterranean. Give the streets and buildings a lived-in, hand-authored appearance. Follow the reference's overall street framing and visual density when footage is available. Create original map geometry.

- Give the main market street a long forward sightline and add two narrower flanking alleys. Connect them through a partially covered market lane with stalls, awnings, tables, crates, and sandbag positions.
- Put a broken courtyard or small plaza near a monumental stone or concrete archway. Include several shallow building interiors, window cut-throughs, and a few clearly readable accessible balconies or low rooftops.
- Give each recognizable spawn zone multiple safe exits. Alternate long-, medium-, and close-range fights. Set cover at useful intervals using low walls, road barriers, carts, vehicles, sandbags, barrels, door frames, and corners. Give routes distinct landmarks and keep open stretches in reach of cover.
- Keep the benchmark layout fixed and reproducible. Use fixed seeds for randomized placement or variation when applicable, including micro-detail, ambient particles, small debris, prop variants, and audio test variation.

### Buildings and street dressing

- Build two- and three-story concrete, plaster, brick, and stucco structures with flat roofs and parapets. Use sun-faded beige, chalky gray, dusty blue, pale peach, muted terracotta, and off-white. Show age through worn plaster, exposed concrete, patches, cracks, chipped corners, discoloration, window grime, bullet marks, and repairs.
- Include recessed doors, barred windows, metal and wooden shutters, rough frames, balconies, railings, ledges, drainpipes, and roof access structures. Add air conditioners, satellite dishes, conduits, utility boxes, lamps, vents, antennas, tanks, and sagging power and communication cables. Dress the upper floors and market with laundry, unbranded fabric flags, hanging rugs, and awnings.
- Vary building width, height, window rhythm, balcony placement, rooftop clutter, color, damage, and grime. Mix open, dark, blocked, boarded, and reflective windows. Furnish visible interior shells with tiled walls, shelves, stairs, curtains, and dust visible in bounced light.
- Place hundreds of details at believable scales. Dress the road with broken and patched asphalt, dust and sand deposits, curbs, drain channels, cracked concrete, pebbles, and weeds. Use Jersey barriers, concrete blocks, sandbag walls, wooden barricades, and metal fences to shape routes and cover.
- Give timber or metal market stalls cloth roofs and dress them with trays, baskets, boxes, sacks, pottery, and unbranded produce. Scatter pallets, crates, plastic containers, metal drums, propane-style cylinders, tires, boards, rubble, bricks, paper, cloth, cans, and discarded household objects. Ground parked utility vehicles, pickups, carts, and damaged street furniture in the streetscape. Add palms, dry shrubs, balcony plants, wall vines, and hardy vegetation.
- Group props where people would use or leave them. Vary rotation, scale, wear, and tint enough to keep repeated assets from forming visible rows or patterns. Choose authored, modular, procedural, or licensed assets and optimize them for the selected runtime.

### Environmental motion

Give awnings, laundry, and hanging fabric layered, slow wind motion. Add subtle cable sway and animate palm fronds and smaller plants. Let dust motes drift through bright shafts and shaded interiors. Occasionally send paper and leaves skittering along the ground. Let nearby muzzle blasts and impacts disturb dust and lightweight debris where appropriate. Use subtle near-ground heat shimmer at a distance above sunlit surfaces.

### Sky and lighting

Set the scene in late morning or midday. Create a bright solar disk, believable atmospheric color and horizon haze, and aerial perspective. Give the sky large banks of slow-moving cumulus with varied scale, bright white sunlit tops, soft gray interiors, and convincing depth and parallax. Let slowly moving cloud shadows make small changes to exterior light.

Use strong directional sunlight, crisp shadows with natural softness, bright sky fill that matches the visible sky, and deep readable shade under balconies and awnings. Maintain believable exposure transitions between sunlight and interiors. Localize dust and use haze to separate distances while retaining color, dark-room detail, and texture in bright plaster. Keep grading neutral and glare restrained.

### Surface and geometry standards

Use physically based materials and believable roughness and normal response. Give plaster, concrete, brick, metal, wood, cloth, roads, glass, sandbags, and weapon surfaces distinct material detail. Combine large color variation with mid-scale cracks and stains and fine surface texture. Maintain consistent texture scale and keep mapped details free of visible stretching. Layer dust, edge wear, water streaks, grime, and impact marks where weather, handling, and combat would put them.

Give glass subdued reflections, dirt, and visible interior darkness. Differentiate painted steel, worn bare edges, anodized weapon parts, dull barrels, and oxidized fixtures by their response to light. Model the forms that affect silhouettes, openings, parallax, and shadows at playable distances, including deep window reveals, weapon controls, and prominent prop attachments. Use bevels or equivalent rounded highlights on hard edges. Reserve fine surface relief for material detail and tune texture density for the nearest playable view. Use suitable levels of detail for distant geometry and keep transitions stable during movement.

## First-Person Weapon and Combat

### Weapon, hands, and optic

- Keep the weapon in view during normal first-person play and give it enough detail for close inspection. Use an unbranded modern M4-style carbine with a detailed receiver, handguard, top rail, barrel, flash hider, magazine well, trigger guard, charging handle, controls, screws, seams, optic mount, and compact tube red-dot optic. - Use detailed tactical gloves and sleeves with convincing folds, seams, stitching, and reinforced knuckles. Maintain hand contact at the grip and fore-end, credible finger placement, and continuous wrists during movement and reload. Tune weapon framing and perspective for a natural first-person view.
- Light the weapon and hands coherently with the scene's sun, sky fill, environmental color, self-shadowing, and nearby muzzle flash. Keep optics, hands, and animated parts clear of the camera near plane and world geometry. Lower the weapon as the player crowds a wall. Preserve sharp motion and keep the optic, fast-moving parts, smoke, and flash free of persistent trails.
- Give the optic a collimated red-dot reticle that stays aligned on the aim direction as the eye moves. Include lens tint, subtle internal reflections, edge occlusion, credible parallax behavior, and brightness adaptation. Move smoothly into and out of ADS with precise eye, sight, and aim alignment, reduced sway, and slight breathing motion.

### Weapon animation and firing behavior

- Layer subtle idle breathing, footstep-synchronized vertical and lateral bob and roll while walking or sprinting, weapon inertia on turns and direction changes, sprint pose, landing compression, jump inertia, crouch transitions, and wall-proximity lowering. Show tactile recoil with a brief camera and FOV impulse, weapon rotation and rearward movement, hand compression, and smooth recovery. Accumulate recoil during automatic fire with subtle seeded variation so repeated shots feel individual.
- Give tactical and empty reloads distinct animation and timing. Show magazine release, extraction and insertion, hand repositioning, any appropriate bolt or charging action for an empty weapon, and a mechanical settle. Tie ammunition changes to the animation and inventory state. Include an inspect animation that reveals weapon details and can be interrupted by combat actions.
- Support semi-auto and full-auto with immediate trigger response, a reproducible base recoil pattern, subtle seeded variation, and natural recovery. Make the first shot tight, penalize moving shots, increase dispersion with sustained fire, and improve stability while crouched or aiming. Keep hits visually and mechanically consistent with aim and with static and moving geometry.
- Support material-based penetration through thin wood, sheet metal, and damaged plaster with sharply reduced fictional damage and different entry and exit feedback. Include plausible shallow-angle ricochets on hard surfaces and occasional visible tracers.

### Shot and impact effects

- Synchronize each shot with a very short white-hot muzzle core, variably shaped orange flame, fast-expanding localized hot-gas distortion, and a brief light response on hands, weapon, smoke, nearby walls, and props. Add brief barrel smoke, a thin smoke trail during sustained fire, and a gradual heat response with increased smoke and modest barrel shimmer. Time bolt movement, casing ejection, and audio to the shot. Simulate casing spin, bounces, and settling with surface-dependent pings, sleep, lifetime limits, and bounded counts.
- Distinguish surface impacts clearly. Use pale dust, small chips, a dark mark, and short-lived fragments on concrete and plaster. Use red dust and fragments on brick. Use sparks, a sharp flash, ringing, and occasional ricochet streaks on metal. Use splinters and dry dust on wood, local cracks or breakage and capped shards on glass, muted fibers or dust on fabric and sandbags, and granular spray with a darker disturbed patch on dirt. Leave persistent bullet decals with distance fading and a bounded pool or equivalent resource limit.

## Physics and Ragdolls

### Simulation and scene queries

- Keep physics behavior stable across render frame rates. Use a fixed or equivalently controlled simulation step with interpolated presentation. A 120 Hz fixed step is a useful baseline where it improves contact stability within the performance budget.
- Provide accurate collision against level geometry and relevant dynamic bodies. Support line or segment tests, sphere and capsule sweeps, closest-surface and overlap queries as needed by the game. Keep the obstruction and contact geometry consistent across locomotion, shots, AI visibility, audio occlusion, and environmental probes.
- Make the player controller sweep and slide along solid surfaces, resolve overlap stably, negotiate stairs and steps, and maintain natural ground contact. Enforce slope limits and controlled steep-slope sliding. Check overhead clearance before standing. Support platforms if any moving walkable geometry is present. Verify thin geometry, corners, steps, low ceilings, and rapid movement for tunneling, wall climbing, snagging, stair jitter, and falling through surfaces.

### Dynamic objects and death physics

- Use collision shapes that fit the gameplay role, including round, box-like, capsule-like, and convex objects as needed. Demand stable contact, bouncing and resting friction, credible crate and barrel stacks, and predictable sleeping and waking. Give cans, boxes, bottles, loose boards, hanging signs, and small rubble believable reactions to shots, collisions, and blasts where appropriate. Prevent fast casings, throwables, and selected debris from passing through walls or floors.
- Transfer the active character pose and linear and angular motion into a constrained, damped ragdoll. Limit joint separation, bend, and twist, and preserve the direction and momentum of the final impact. Solve against terrain, obstacles, and important dynamic objects without explosive joints, persistent jitter, or graphic injury. Put settled bodies to sleep and retire them after a controlled lifetime.
- Cap, sleep, pool, or recycle temporary bodies so long firefights and consecutive rounds remain stable and responsive.
